\transition{FADE IN:}
\sceneheading{EXT. MOUNTAINSIDE - PRE-DAWN}
\action{Cold. Still. Stars fading out of a graying sky.}
\action{AL-BIRUNI (30s, spare build, exact and unhurried in his movements) climbs the last stretch of the slope alone, carrying a wooden instrument case.}
\action{A YOUNG SHEPHERD (14), already up with his flock, watches him from a distance. Not wary. Curious, in the way of someone who has seen this before and still doesn't understand it.}
\action{Biruni finds his spot. Sets up a homemade quadrant on a modified tripod, working from memory rather than instruction. He is not hurrying, though the light is changing fast.}
\action{He sights along the instrument toward the horizon. Marks something on a wax tablet with a stylus. Waits. Sights again.}
\action{The Shepherd drifts closer, unable to help himself.}
\charcue{SHEPHERD}
\begin{dialogue}
What are you looking for?\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{BIRUNI}
\begin{dialogue}
\parenthetical{not looking up}
Nothing. I'm measuring what's already there.\\
\end{dialogue}
\action{He crouches, does not show the boy the instrument's face. Instead he works arithmetic into the dirt with a stick --- an angle, a known height, a formula worked through by feel.}
\charcue{SHEPHERD}
\begin{dialogue}
Measuring what.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{BIRUNI}
\begin{dialogue}
How big the thing is that we're both standing on.\\
\end{dialogue}
\action{The boy looks down at the mountain under his own feet. Then out at the horizon. He is plainly unconvinced that lines scratched in dirt could know something his eyes can't.}
\action{He doesn't argue the point. He just turns back toward his flock.}
\action{Biruni looks at the number in the dirt for a long moment --- no triumph in it, just a private, settled satisfaction. He packs the quadrant away.}
\transition{CUT TO:}
\sceneheading{INT. OBSERVATORY WORKSHOP, KHWAREZM - DAY}
\action{Cramped, well-used. Astrolabes in various states of construction hang from the walls and lie across worktables. Biruni works beside two APPRENTICES on a half-finished astrolabe, running a finger along its rete, feeling for error before the numbers confirm it.}
\charcue{BIRUNI}
\begin{dialogue}
This star's arm is wrong.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{APPRENTICE 1}
\begin{dialogue}
It matches the tables we copied.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{BIRUNI}
\begin{dialogue}
Then the tables we copied are wrong.\\
\parenthetical{beat}
Copying isn't the same as checking.\\
\end{dialogue}
\action{He recalculates on the spot, quick and assured --- the ease of a man for whom this kind of work costs him nothing.}
\action{The COURT ASTROLOGER (50s, unhurried, comfortable in every room he enters) drifts in, glancing over the shop with the easy familiarity of someone who's never once doubted his own usefulness here.}
\charcue{ASTROLOGER}
\begin{dialogue}
The stars are generous today.\\
\parenthetical{beat}
Or they will be, once I've finished with them.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{BIRUNI}
\begin{dialogue}
The stars don't need your help.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{ASTROLOGER}
\begin{dialogue}
\parenthetical{not unkindly}
No. But the ruler does.\\
\end{dialogue}
\action{Biruni doesn't dignify it with a response. The Astrologer doesn't need one --- he wasn't really asking.}
\transition{CUT TO:}
\sceneheading{EXT. KHWAREZM, CITY STREET - DAY}
\action{Ordinary life. A market. A canal catching the light. The observatory's dome visible above the rooftops in the background --- unremarkable, everyday, the way a thing looks right before you learn you're about to lose it.}
\transition{CUT TO:}
\sceneheading{INT. BIRUNI'S QUARTERS - NIGHT}
\action{Biruni at a low table, notebooks fanned around him in careful disorder. Among ordinary astronomical notes, he lifts out a small clay tablet --- Sanskrit characters, copied years earlier from a merchant's ledger.}
\action{He can't read it. He's kept it anyway.}
\action{Beside it, a page of his own guesses at a few of the characters --- visibly revised more than once, crossed out, re-annotated in a different ink. He looks at the page for a while. Doesn't write anything new tonight. Sets it aside, carefully, like something left open on purpose.}
\transition{CUT TO:}
\sceneheading{EXT. KHWAREZM, OBSERVATORY COURTYARD - DAY}
\action{Smoke on the horizon, thin at first. A sound underneath the ordinary noise of the courtyard --- distant, wrong. A horn.}
\action{People start moving with purpose. The Astrologer crosses the courtyard at a near-run, all his earlier ease gone.}
\charcue{ASTROLOGER}
\begin{dialogue}
Ghazni's army. They're not stopping at the gates.\\
\end{dialogue}
\action{Biruni doesn't run toward shelter. He runs for the workshop.}
\transition{CUT TO:}
\sceneheading{INT. OBSERVATORY WORKSHOP - CONTINUOUS}
\action{Chaos outside; inside, Biruni is eerily, terribly focused. Saddlebags --- clearly not enough room for everything the room holds. He has to choose.}
\action{He takes: the portable astrolabe. The notebooks. The clay tablet.}
\action{He has to leave: a larger instrument, one we recognize from earlier in the scene --- one of the astrolabes he was correcting with the apprentices. He touches its edge once. Leaves it.}
\action{GHAZNAVID SOLDIERS enter --- not gratuitous, but clearly here to take inventory of a place, not to ask permission. An OFFICER looks over the workshop the way you'd appraise livestock.}
\charcue{OFFICER}
\begin{dialogue}
You're the instrument-maker.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{BIRUNI}
\begin{dialogue}
I'm an astronomer.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{OFFICER}
\begin{dialogue}
\parenthetical{already turning away}
You're coming with the instruments.\\
\end{dialogue}
\transition{CUT TO:}
\sceneheading{EXT. ROAD TO GHAZNI - DAY}
\action{A displaced column moves under guard --- scholars, craftsmen, crated goods. Not quite prisoners. Not remotely free.}
\action{Biruni walks near the rear, notebook open even while walking, sketching a star position from memory. Correcting a figure without breaking stride. The work does not stop. Only where it happens has changed.}
\transition{CUT TO:}
\sceneheading{INT. GHAZNI COURT, ANTECHAMBER - DAY}
\action{Biruni waits in a line of requisitioned scholars and craftsmen --- a mixed, uneasy group. A CHAMBERLAIN moves down the line assigning roles with the flat efficiency of a man taking inventory.}
\charcue{CHAMBERLAIN}
\begin{dialogue}
\parenthetical{not looking up}
Instruments and tables. You'll work under the court astronomer.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{BIRUNI}
\begin{dialogue}
I'd like to request something else.\\
\end{dialogue}
\action{The Chamberlain looks up. This doesn't happen.}
\charcue{CHAMBERLAIN}
\begin{dialogue}
Go on.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{BIRUNI}
\begin{dialogue}
When the campaign goes into India --- I want to go. Not with the army. Ahead of it, or beside it. It doesn't matter which. I want to learn the language. Not through translators. Myself.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{CHAMBERLAIN}
\begin{dialogue}
Why would you want that.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{BIRUNI}
\begin{dialogue}
Because a number translated by someone else isn't the same number. I want the number itself.\\
\end{dialogue}
\action{The Chamberlain doesn't understand this. Doesn't pretend to. But it's strange enough that he's not willing to decide it on his own authority.}
\charcue{CHAMBERLAIN}
\begin{dialogue}
That's not mine to grant.\\
\end{dialogue}
\transition{CUT TO:}
\sceneheading{INT. GHAZNI COURT, AUDIENCE HALL - DAY}
\action{Biruni stands before MAHMUD OF GHAZNI --- not a monster, not a caricature. A man who has spent his life evaluating what things are for.}
\charcue{MAHMUD}
\begin{dialogue}
You're the one who wants to learn their language before we've finished taking their cities.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{BIRUNI}
\begin{dialogue}
Yes.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{MAHMUD}
\begin{dialogue}
Most men want the gold first.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{BIRUNI}
\begin{dialogue}
I don't have a use for gold I can't check.\\
\end{dialogue}
\action{Mahmud studies him. This isn't admiration exactly --- more like the mild interest of a man who's found an odd tool that might still turn out to be useful.}
\charcue{MAHMUD}
\begin{dialogue}
Go, then. Learn what you like. Bring back whatever's useful.\\
\end{dialogue}
\charcue{BIRUNI}
\begin{dialogue}
I intend to bring back what's true. Whether or not it's useful is your problem, not mine.\\
\end{dialogue}
\action{A beat. Mahmud doesn't react much either way --- he's already moving his attention to the next matter in the room, which is itself the point. To power, this whole exchange is a rounding error.}
\transition{CUT TO:}
\sceneheading{EXT. GHAZNI, CITY GATE - DAY}
\action{Biruni joins a column heading toward the Indus --- traveling in the protection of Mahmud's army, but toward a different purpose than the soldiers around him.}
\action{He carries the same saddlebags from the workshop. Takes out the clay tablet once. Looks at it. Puts it away.}
\action{WIDE SHOT: the column moving out, into terrain none of them have mapped the way Biruni means to map it.}
\transition{HARD CUT TO BLACK.}
